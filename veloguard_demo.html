One entrance, one decision

A campus or estate manager hears that vehicles enter a one-way lane in reverse. Should they inspect the sign, post a marshal at a particular time, or leave the arrangement alone? VeloGuard produces a reviewed report that helps make that decision.

The proposed site

Pattanagere / Mysore Road service lane is a candidate. Site control, posted direction, hours, exceptions, visibility and access are UNVERIFIED. A trajectory cannot establish a rider's motive.

What the manager receives

A security supervisor reviews candidate clips for one defined entrance. A periodic report lists unique reviewed events, minutes observed per time bin, downtime, uncertainties and a proposed decision. It is compared with existing CCTV review, staff observation and a simpler signage change.

What has been tested

Synthetic desk scenes only. A recording played later is a replay. Real road recording needs independent scoring before any road number is quoted. Until then: "Road performance is unknown."

Limits of the product

No trained vehicle classifier. No plate recognition. No driver identification. No roadside signalling. No police integration. No fines. Buyer, price, purchase authority, retention and field performance are unvalidated. Mobility impact is unmeasured.

Evidence: how we score the detector

Mark every real crossing in the untouched original before looking at any flag. One matched pair is a TP. An unmatched flag, including a second flag on the same passage, is an FP. An unmatched real crossing is an FN.

Blind-label protocol

Preserve an authorised continuous original. Mark a development segment; reserve the rest untouched. Label before seeing flags. A second reviewer checks ambiguous cases. Freeze labels, then reveal flags. If retuned after seeing test errors, that interval becomes development footage.

Why the detector can be wrong

Shadow, pedestrian or reflection · split blobs and ID switches · stopped vehicles and camera jitter · bends and authorised diversions · rain, darkness, exposure. Use the scenario buttons in the demo to reproduce several of these.

Worst failure to show

The Split vehicle scenario: one object becomes two patches and two flags for one passage. First flag is TP, second is FP. The pedestrian and glare scenarios are further false positives. State whether a fix was tested on development footage.

Mobility effect: timing against matched controls

Delay is unmeasured on the road. This calculator shows the method on invented synthetic numbers. The result is an association under observed conditions, not crash prevention, fuel or carbon saving.

Inputs synthetic example

Result

Signed difference = observed transit seconds − matched median control seconds. Keep negative and positive values. Sum across distinct affected vehicles once per event. Unit: vehicle-seconds per event. Never select only the fastest controls.

Interpretation for judges

Density, signals and road geometry can explain a difference. Demonstrating avoided delay would need an approved intervention comparison with matched windows, traffic volume, exposure and confounding checked. Current result: unmeasured. This is a mobility project with an indirect climate hypothesis.

Say / do not say

If the live demonstration fails

One restart attempt, at most ten seconds. LED alone fails → say it is offline and continue with the screen. Camera fails → "The live camera has failed. This is a recording of our earlier tabletop run." Label the replay. Playback fails → show the saved CSV row and still. Both missing → show the labelled schematic, say the demo did not run, and walk through the expected flow. Never toggle the LED manually and call it automatic detection.

Last line for an unscored site: "This is a tabletop simulation. Road performance is unknown. The grant would let us test a permitted entrance against independent counts and ask its manager for a decision."
